\subsection{Cross-validated Surrogate Performance}
\label{sec:cv_results}

Table~\ref{tab:cv_metrics} collects the leave-one-out error metrics over
$n_H=97$ high-fidelity designs. At each wavelength, NRMSE is the RMSE across
held-out designs divided by the population standard deviation of the
corresponding high-fidelity reference values ($\mathrm{ddof}=0$). The table
reports arithmetic means of these per-wavelength NRMSE values, over either
all $195$ bins or the indicated instrument band. These averages are distinct
from a single pooled RMSE divided by the standard deviation of all
$n_H\times m$ reference values.

Under mean per-wavelength NRMSE, the ordering is Model~3 ($@@model3-mf_all_plain@@$),
the PCA--PCE reference of \citet{Nussbaumer2026} ($@@cn_all_plain@@$),
Model~1 ($@@model1-mf_all_plain@@$), the single-fidelity GPBoost baseline
($@@model3-sf_all_plain@@$), and the single-fidelity GP baseline
($@@model1-sf_all_plain@@$). Model~3 improves on the reference by
$@@improvement_over_reference_percent@@\%$.

To compare errors at the design level, we retain the same wavelength-specific
standard deviations and form one score per held-out design,
\[
 q_i = \left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}
 \left(\frac{\widehat{y}_{ij}^{(-i)}-y_{ij}}{s_j}\right)^2\right]^{1/2},
 \qquad
 s_j = \left[\frac{1}{n_H}\sum_{i=1}^{n_H}
 (y_{ij}-\bar y_j)^2\right]^{1/2}.
\]
Here $q_i$ is the root-mean-square standardized residual across wavelengths;
it differs from dividing a spectrum's RMSE by that spectrum's own standard
deviation. Two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank comparisons use the $97$ paired
scores, with Holm adjustment across the four comparisons below. The reported
standard deviations of paired differences use $n_H-1$ in their denominator.

Model~3 improves on Model~1 on $@@pair0_wins@@$ of $97$ designs, with a mean
paired difference $q_i^{(1)}-q_i^{(3)}$ of $@@pair0_mean@@$
(sd $@@pair0_sd@@$; unadjusted $p=@@pair0_wilcoxon_p@@$,
adjusted $p=@@pair0_wilcoxon_p_holm@@$). Model~1 improves on the PCA--PCE
reference on only $@@pair1_wins@@$ designs: the mean paired difference
favours the reference by $@@pair1_mean@@$
(unadjusted $p=@@pair1_wilcoxon_p@@$,
adjusted $p=@@pair1_wilcoxon_p_holm@@$).
Model~3 improves on the reference on $@@pair2_wins@@$ designs
(unadjusted and adjusted $p=@@pair2_wilcoxon_p@@$), and on the
single-fidelity GP on $@@pair3_wins@@$ designs
(unadjusted $p=@@pair3_wilcoxon_p@@$,
adjusted $p=@@pair3_wilcoxon_p_holm@@$).
Thus the standardized design-level scores favour Model~3 over Model~1 and
the single-fidelity GP, while the comparison with PCA--PCE remains
inconclusive. These tests are exploratory because the leave-one-out training
sets overlap; pairing avoids treating wavelength bins as independent
replicates but does not make the fitted surrogates independent across folds.

MAE gives a different ordering among the remaining architectures. Model~3
leads at $@@model3_mae_ppm@@$ ppm, whereas Model~1 reaches
$@@model1-mf_mae_ppm_plain@@$ ppm and is thereby worse than the
single-fidelity GP baseline ($@@model1-sf_mae_ppm_plain@@$ ppm).
For Model~3, mean per-wavelength NRMSE is similar in the NIRCam and MIRI bands
($@@nircam_mean_nrmse@@$ and $@@miri_mean_nrmse@@$, respectively), while MAE
increases from $@@below3_mae_ppm@@$ ppm for bin centres below $3\,\mu$m to
$@@above10_mae_ppm@@$ ppm for bin centres strictly above $10\,\mu$m.
Over those same slices, the mean high-fidelity eclipse depth increases from
$@@below3_hf_mean_ppm@@$ to $@@above10_hf_mean_ppm@@$ ppm, a factor of
$@@hf_amplitude_ratio@@$. Long-wavelength bins therefore account for much of
the absolute error without having larger band-averaged standardized error.

The best surrogate reaches $\mathrm{MAE}=@@model3_mae_ppm@@$ ppm, or
$@@model3_mae_over_sigma@@\,\bar\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}$, where
$\bar\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}=@@sigma_obs_ppm@@$ ppm is obtained by averaging
the lower and upper observational error bars within each bin and then across
bins. Its per-bin MAE is below this bin's observational uncertainty in
$@@bins_mae_below_sigma@@$ of $195$ bins; its per-bin RMSE is below that
uncertainty in $@@bins_rmse_below_sigma@@$ bins. Emulation error is therefore
comparable to the observational error scale. This supports retaining emulator
uncertainty in the retrieval likelihood
\eqref{eq:inversion-surrogate-likelihood}; the MAE comparison alone does not
establish the magnitude or calibration of that variance contribution.

Squared error is concentrated in a subset of wavelength bins: the worst
$10$ bins account for $@@sse10@@\%$ of the total, the worst $5$ for
$@@sse5@@\%$, and the worst $20$ for $@@sse20@@\%$.
The five largest squared-error contributions occur between
$10.5$ and $11.75\,\mu$m. The top ten also include shorter-wavelength bins,
including one at $3.9875\,\mu$m. The largest per-wavelength standardized
error instead occurs at $@@peak_nrmse_wavelength@@\,\mu$m, where
$\mathrm{NRMSE}_j=@@peak_nrmse@@$
(Figure~\ref{fig:nrmse_lambda}). Absolute-error concentration and
standardized-error maxima therefore identify different parts of the spectrum.

Across design points, the spectrum-level relative $L_2$ error,
$\|\widehat{\bm y}_i-\bm y_i\|_2/\|\bm y_i\|_2$, has median
$@@relative_l2_median@@$, mean $@@relative_l2_mean@@$, empirical skewness
$+@@relative_l2_skewness@@$, and maximum $@@relative_l2_max@@$. This is a
separate metric from standard-deviation-normalized RMSE.
Relative $L_2$ error correlates with oxygen abundance (Spearman
$\rho=+@@oxygen_rho@@$, $p\simeq 1.0\times10^{-5}$); the other inputs have
$|\rho|<0.08$ and $p>0.48$. The eight worst designs have mean oxygen
abundance $+@@worst8_oxygen_standard_deviations@@$ population standard
deviations above the design mean. These diagnostics associate larger errors
with the high-oxygen-abundance part of the design domain, without establishing
a causal explanation or demonstrating that errors are confined to that region.
